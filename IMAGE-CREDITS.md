# Image credits and licensing

## Photographs

All photographs on this site come from **[Pexels](https://www.pexels.com)** and are used under
the [Pexels License](https://www.pexels.com/license/): free for commercial and non-commercial
use, no attribution required, modification allowed. They are credited here anyway, because it
is the decent thing to do and because you will want to know where they came from.

Every photograph was picked and reviewed by eye to make sure the people shown are **Indian or
South Asian**, per the brief. Two of the consultant portraits and one facility photo are of
South Asian people photographed in Dhaka and in a Canadian pharmacy respectively; if you want
strictly Indian-shot photography, replace those three.

Re-download and re-crop everything at any time with:

```bash
npm run photos
```

The exact Pexels photo id for every file is listed in `scripts/fetch-photos.mjs`. To view an
original, open `https://www.pexels.com/photo/<id>/`.

## Illustrations

The **logo**, the **favicon** and the **CTA band background** are original SVGs generated by
`scripts/generate-images.mjs` (`npm run images`). They are yours to use and modify freely —
nothing in them is licensed from anyone else. Every other image on the site is a photograph.

The Contact page uses a live Google Maps embed rather than an image; point
`CONTACT.mapQuery` in `client/src/lib/site.js` at your real address.

The generator also produces SVG portraits of Indian doctors and patients. Those are no longer
displayed (the photographs took their place) but are kept as an offline fallback: delete the
`.jpg` files and re-run `npm run use-photos` and the site falls back to the illustrations.

## Important before you publish

These are **stock photographs of models, not your staff or your patients.**

1. Do not present a stock photograph as a named real doctor to patients making a medical
   decision. Replace every consultant portrait with a photograph of the actual consultant,
   taken with their consent.
2. The same applies to testimonials. Real patient feedback needs the patient's written
   consent, and the photograph should be of that patient or omitted entirely.
3. Some stock photographs contain incidental third-party branding. Two were handled for this
   reason: the pharmacy-brand badge in `dr-jaswinder-singh-chadha.jpg` was cropped out, and the
   original About photo was replaced because it showed another hospital's name on the wall.
   Check any photo you add for logos, name badges and signage before publishing it.
4. If you swap in AI-generated images instead, see `AI-IMAGE-PROMPTS.md`, and do not present
   AI-generated faces as real named people either.
